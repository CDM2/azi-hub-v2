# 01_RESEARCH_PROBLEM_v2.md

## 1. Research Problem Statement
The fundamental research problem of the CAT program is to determine whether there is a valuable, defensible, and technically viable control point for creating, transforming, operating, coordinating, and continuously improving increasingly AI-native organizations. The research must remain equally capable of discovering opportunities involving greenfield creation, brownfield transformation, single organizations, multi-organization portfolios, or a common underlying platform spanning some combination of these. The research must not assume that such a control point inherently exists, nor prematurely commit to a specific product architecture or software category.

## 2. Founder Ambition vs. Actual Research Question
The initial founder ambition hypothesized the necessity of an "Agentic OS" or an "Enterprise AI Change Management Operating System" to orchestrate this transition using centralized architectures like a top-down "Conductor Engine" and an "Enterprise Brain". 

However, the actual central research question is: 
**"Where, if anywhere, is the highest-leverage and most defensible control point in the emerging AI software and organizational stack for enabling organizations to become increasingly AI-native, and what customer problem would justify owning that control point?"**

The research must preserve the possibility that it concludes no distinct CAT or "Agentic OS" category should exist at all.

## 3. Strategic Hypothesis Space
The program must maintain a wide aperture regarding the eventual product category. The research must evaluate the following potential strategic positions as unresolved hypotheses to determine where durable value actually accumulates. These hypotheses span different kinds of questions and are organized accordingly below: what layer of the stack might own the value (product/control-point), how the system might be built (architectural), and how it might be sold and delivered (business-model/delivery-model). Grouping them this way does not imply any hypothesis is more or less likely than another — it only clarifies that they are not directly comparable to one another as stated.

### 3a. Product / Control-Point Hypotheses
*   **Organizational Capability / Composition Layer:** A system that determines what capabilities an organization requires to accomplish its objectives and dynamically composes those capabilities from some combination of humans, agents, software, workflows, data, external services, and purchased tools. (This explicitly distinguishes organizational capabilities from static job titles; it does not assume an organization needs fixed executive-role agents such as a CEO/CMO/CTO).
*   **Organizational Intelligence Layer:** A passive data fabric that maps corporate context.
*   **Transformation Platform:** A system dedicated solely to managing the lifecycle of AI adoption.
*   **AI-Native Company Operating System:** A comprehensive platform replacing legacy systems.
*   **Enterprise AI Control Point:** A middleware boundary acting as a control point between an organization's existing systems and external AI agents/models.
*   **Organizational Memory/Knowledge Layer:** A centralized repository maintaining context across agents.
*   **Governance/Policy Layer:** An enforcement mechanism for security and compliance.
*   **Integration/Modernization Layer:** A progressive system wrapping non-idempotent legacy APIs with natural language interfaces.

### 3b. Architectural Hypotheses
*   **Agent Infrastructure:** Providing the underlying rails for building and hosting agents.
*   **Orchestration/Control Plane:** A routing and coordination layer for multi-agent workflows.
*   **Optimization/Self-Improvement Layer:** A system that passively analyzes and improves automated workflows.

### 3c. Business-Model / Delivery-Model Hypotheses
*   **Hybrid Software + Implementation Platform:** A dual-motion business model blending software with human-in-the-loop implementation specialists.

## 4. Greenfield/Brownfield Framework
The research must treat the operational starting point of the target customer as a primary variable, investigating both as distinct but potentially related environments. 
*   **Greenfield:** The process of autonomous venture creation, building entirely new AI-native organizations from scratch without legacy constraints.
*   **Brownfield:** The process of legacy-to-AI-native transformation, integrating with existing systems of record and established corporate structures.

## 5. Single-Org/Multi-Org Framework
The research must also explicitly investigate the scale and boundary of the system's operational deployment.
*   **Single Organization:** The platform operates within, creates, or transforms one distinct organization.
*   **Multi-Organization:** The platform operates across a portfolio or holding-company structure, managing or creating multiple organizations simultaneously.

## 6. 2x2 Hypothesis Matrix
The intersection of these frameworks yields a four-quadrant hypothesis space. The research does not conclude which quadrant is best; rather, it aims to discover what evidence is needed to compare them and determine if they represent different products, customer segments, go-to-market motions, or applications of a common underlying control plane.

| | Single Organization | Multi-Organization |
|---|---|---|
| **Greenfield** | Create/run one new AI-native organization. | Create/run a portfolio of new AI-native organizations. |
| **Brownfield** | Transform one existing organization into an AI-native organization. | Transform/manage a portfolio of existing organizations toward AI-native operation. |

## 7. Technical Research Questions
*   **Question:** What architectural approaches—including centralized topological knowledge graphs, decentralized progressive API wrapping, event-driven stream processing, or other paradigms—are most effective for mapping and operating an AI-native organization?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Evaluation of candidate architectures across dimensions including task success, reliability, latency, cost, security, maintainability, recovery/fault tolerance, scalability, interoperability, and organizational fit.
*   **Question:** What is the optimal coordination methodology for multi-agent or human-agent systems, considering paradigms ranging from top-down dynamic context engines to decentralized peer-to-peer meshes, Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), or serverless execution environments?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Quantitative and qualitative benchmarking of routing and coordination layers across dimensions such as task success, latency, fault-tolerance, scalability, and cost.
*   **Question:** What mechanisms, if any, are most effective for dynamically discovering, mapping, and ingesting unstructured institutional knowledge and shadow IT?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Comparative performance data of automated ingestion tools, manual blueprinting approaches, and hybrid methods measured against dimensions of accuracy, security, scalability, and maintainability.

## 8. Customer / Job To Be Done Research Questions
*   **Question:** What painful, valuable problems are buyers actually paying to solve, and do these center on unit cost reduction, security risk mitigation, institutional memory retention, or other yet-unidentified objectives?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Direct customer interviews, budget allocation analysis, and the specific KPIs tied to enterprise AI or venture-creation procurement.
*   **Question:** Which quadrant in the Greenfield/Brownfield x Single-Org/Multi-Org matrix represents the most lucrative or frictionless initial go-to-market wedge?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Sales cycle velocity data, customer acquisition costs, and contract value comparisons across target segments (e.g., venture studios vs. established regulated enterprises vs. physical operations).

## 9. Economic Research Questions
*   **Question:** Does onboarding organizations onto an AI-native operating model require intensive human-in-the-loop implementation specialists, and how does this affect viability and SaaS margins?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Financial modeling of the gross margin profile and implementation costs of hybrid software-and-services motions versus pure-play SaaS deployments.
*   **Question:** Does the successful deployment of the platform create reusable organizational schemas, cross-company playbook sharing, or network effects that lower the cost of subsequent customer deployments?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Tracking the percentage of structural rollout templates, code, or prompts successfully reused across disparate clients or internal portfolio ventures.

## 10. Moat / Control-Point Research Questions
*   **Question:** Where could durable value accumulate if foundational models natively incorporate large-context reasoning, tool use, memory, and routing?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Evaluating the feature roadmaps of foundational model providers to map the commoditization frontier against candidate control points.
*   **Question:** Can the process of secure embedding, human correction, or continuous optimization generate proprietary data exhaust that creates a defensive moat?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Assessment of whether telemetry data (e.g., human-in-the-loop approvals, prompt optimizations, or workflow execution logs) yields unique, non-replicable algorithmic improvements.

## 11. Transformation / Onboarding Research Questions
*   **Question:** What is required to move an organization from its current state to an increasingly AI-native state, and does this require fundamentally different operating models for greenfield versus brownfield starting points?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Case studies and pilot metrics measuring adoption friction, time-to-value, and churn rates of legacy ingestion methods versus zero-to-one organizational creation.
*   **Question:** Are established change management frameworks (e.g., McKinsey's *Rewired*) applicable to AI-native transformations, or do they require entirely new methodologies based on complex adaptive systems or algorithmic management psychology?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Comparative analysis of organizational failure modes when utilizing traditional structured rollout plans versus dynamic, adaptive AI capability composition.

## 12. Security / Governance / Enterprise Readiness Questions
*   **Question:** What specific security and governance requirements (e.g., edge-verified cryptographic RBAC, SOP-tied hallucination controls, cross-border data sovereignty) are actual prerequisites for commercial adoption, versus theoretical best practices?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Audit logs of compliance team rejections, SOC 2 compliance gap analyses, and common IT procurement hurdles for VPC or on-premise deployments.
*   **Question:** How does an automated system safely resolve conflicting objectives or handle interactions with non-idempotent legacy APIs without cascading systemic failures?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Evaluation of deterministic rollback mechanisms and failure rates at which human-in-the-loop approval degrades into notification fatigue.

## 13. Build-vs-Buy Research Questions
*   **Question:** To what extent will hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP), major CRM/ERP incumbents, or iPaaS vendors utilize their distribution advantages to natively provide "good enough" orchestration and capability composition, rendering custom control points redundant?
    *   *Evidence Needed:* Feature parity analysis between native AI offerings from legacy enterprise providers, hyperscalers, and specialized multi-agent frameworks.

## 14. Falsification Criteria

### A. Program-Level Falsification
What evidence would suggest there is no sufficiently valuable, defensible, technically viable CAT opportunity across the broader hypothesis space?
*   *Evidence:* Hyperscalers and foundational model providers entirely consume both the orchestration layer and the organizational capability-composition layer natively, leaving no viable gap or willingness-to-pay for an independent, third-party control point.
*   *Evidence:* The human implementation and manual blueprinting cost required to transition an organization into an AI-native state permanently exceeds the economic value generated by the automation, rendering any business model structurally unprofitable.
*   *Evidence:* No sufficiently painful, budgeted customer problem exists for an independent layer, even if the technology is feasible. This is a distinct failure mode from the two above: CAT can fail because the technology is unnecessary, because incumbents absorb it, or simply because customers do not care enough to buy it, and the research must test for all three separately.

### B. Hypothesis-Level Falsification
What evidence would invalidate specific product categories, quadrants, architectures, or business models?
*   *Routing/Orchestration Thesis:* Materially weakened or invalidated, depending on the extent of commoditization, if off-the-shelf LLMs natively resolve multi-agent coordination, serverless execution, and memory management at the base-model layer. A native model-layer capability does not automatically eliminate a higher-level orchestration/control product if governance, state, integrations, policy, observability, or organizational semantics still matter — the research must assess degree of commoditization rather than treat this as a binary outcome.
*   *Brownfield Integration Mesh Thesis:* Invalidated if major CRM and ERP incumbents lock down their API ecosystems, effectively blocking third-party middleware from reading or writing core operational data.
*   *Executive-Role Agent Thesis:* Invalidated if empirical testing proves that dynamic, ephemeral capability composition (or deterministic workflows) drastically outperforms fixed, static "CEO/CMO" agent personas in task success and latency.
*   *Self-Optimization Thesis:* Invalidated if evaluation datasets consistently cause the system to overfit to narrow metrics or if human-in-the-loop approvals trigger unsustainable notification fatigue rather than compounding improvements.

## 15. Questions that MUST Remain Unresolved Until Evidence is Gathered
*   Whether a common underlying platform can span both Greenfield and Brownfield environments, or if they demand fundamentally different products.
*   Which quadrant of the 2x2 matrix represents the most viable initial commercial wedge.
*   Whether the final architecture should rely on centralized graphs, decentralized meshes, static workflows, or dynamic capability composition.
*   Whether the eventual business model is purely SaaS, consumption-based, or a hybrid software-and-services model.

## 16. Research Boundaries (Explicit Non-Assumptions)
*   We are explicitly **NOT assuming** that a distinct CAT product or "Agentic OS" control point should exist.
*   We are explicitly **NOT assuming** that an organization requires fixed, human-mimicking executive-role agents (e.g., CEO, CMO, CTO) as part of its capability composition.
*   We are explicitly **NOT assuming** that the McKinsey *Rewired* framework represents the correct or optimal path for AI change management.
*   We are explicitly **NOT assuming** that any historical feature of AZI Hub or Conducting AI (e.g., Omni-Chat interfaces, North Star injection, or top-down Conductor engines) is required for the final solution.
